Part 1

What Is an Exponent?

An exponent tells us how many times a base is used as a factor.

aⁿ = a × a × a × ... × a

The base is a. The exponent is n.

Example: 2⁴ = 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 = 16
Remember: The exponent does NOT mean base × exponent. It tells you how many copies of the base are multiplied.
Part 2

Law 1 — Product of Powers

When multiplying powers with the same base, keep the base and add the exponents.

aᵐ × aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ
Example: 2³ × 2⁴ = 2³⁺⁴ = 2⁷
Another: x⁵ × x² = x⁷
Think: Multiplication → Add the exponents.
Part 3

Law 2 — Quotient of Powers

When dividing powers with the same base, keep the base and subtract the exponents.

aᵐ ÷ aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ
Example: 5⁶ ÷ 5² = 5⁶⁻² = 5⁴
Another: x⁸ ÷ x³ = x⁵
Think: Division → Subtract the exponents.
Part 4

Law 3 — Power of a Power

When a power is raised to another power, multiply the exponents.

(aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐⁿ
Example: (2³)² = 2³×² = 2⁶
Another: (x⁴)³ = x¹²
Think: Power outside a power → Multiply the exponents.
Part 5

Law 4 — Power of a Product

An exponent outside parentheses applies to every factor inside.

(ab)ⁿ = aⁿbⁿ
Example: (2x)³ = 2³x³ = 8x³
Another: (3ab)² = 9a²b²
Be careful: (a + b)² is NOT a² + b². The product rule applies to multiplication, not addition.
Part 6

Law 5 — Power of a Quotient

An exponent outside a fraction applies to both the numerator and denominator.

(a/b)ⁿ = aⁿ/bⁿ
Example: (2/3)³ = 2³/3³ = 8/27
Another: (x/y)² = x²/y²
Part 7

Law 6 — Zero Exponent

Any nonzero number raised to the zero power equals 1.

a⁰ = 1    (a ≠ 0)
7⁰ = 1
x⁰ = 1   (when x ≠ 0)
The zero exponent does not make the answer zero. It makes the answer 1.
Part 8

Law 7 — Negative Exponents

A negative exponent means the reciprocal of the positive exponent.

a⁻ⁿ = 1/aⁿ
2⁻³ = 1/2³ = 1/8
x⁻⁴ = 1/x⁴
Think: Negative exponent → Move the factor across the fraction bar and make the exponent positive.
Part 9

Combining Laws

Some expressions require more than one law. Work carefully from one step to the next.

Example:
(x² × x³)²
= (x⁵)²   Product of Powers
= x¹⁰   Power of a Power
Example:
x⁷ ÷ x² = x⁷⁻² = x⁵
Strategy: Identify the operation first, choose the matching law, then simplify one step at a time.

Final Review

🧠 Remember the Laws

Multiply same bases
ADD exponents
aᵐ · aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ
Divide same bases
SUBTRACT exponents
aᵐ / aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ
Power of a power
MULTIPLY exponents
(aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐⁿ
Power of a product
Apply to every factor
(ab)ⁿ = aⁿbⁿ
Zero exponent
Nonzero base → 1
a⁰ = 1
Negative exponent
Use the reciprocal
a⁻ⁿ = 1/aⁿ
