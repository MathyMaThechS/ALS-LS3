Part 1

Perfect Squares & Square Roots

When you multiply a number by itself, you get a perfect square.

n² = n × n
1² = 1
√1 = 1
2² = 4
√4 = 2
3² = 9
√9 = 3
4² = 16
√16 = 4
5² = 25
√25 = 5
6² = 36
√36 = 6
7² = 49
√49 = 7
8² = 64
√64 = 8
9² = 81
√81 = 9
10² = 100
√100 = 10
Example: A square garden has side 8 m. Area = 8² = 64 m². If area = 49 m², side = √49 = 7 m.
Remember: The square root (√) asks: "What number times itself gives this number?"
Note: √25 = 5 only. We don't write −5 unless asked for all roots.
Part 2

Perfect Cubes & Cube Roots

When you multiply a number by itself three times, you get a perfect cube.

n³ = n × n × n
1³ = 1
∛1 = 1
2³ = 8
∛8 = 2
3³ = 27
∛27 = 3
4³ = 64
∛64 = 4
5³ = 125
∛125 = 5
6³ = 216
∛216 = 6
7³ = 343
∛343 = 7
8³ = 512
∛512 = 8
9³ = 729
∛729 = 9
10³ = 1000
∛1000 = 10
Example: A cube box has side 3 cm. Volume = 3³ = 27 cm³. If volume = 125 cm³, side = ∛125 = 5 cm.
Remember: The cube root symbol ∛ has a small 3 called the index. It means "what number multiplied three times gives this?"
Part 3

Basics of Radicals

A radical is an expression that uses a root, such as square root or cube root.

index → ⁿ√ a ← radicand

The symbol √

Tells which root: 2 = square, 3 = cube

Number inside the radical symbol
Square Root

Estimating Roots

For numbers that are NOT perfect squares or cubes, we can estimate between which two roots they fall.

Example: √20
16 < 20 < 25  →  √16 < √20 < √25  →  4 < √20 < 5
Example: ∛30
27 < 30 < 64  →  ∛27 < ∛30 < ∛64  →  3 < ∛30 < 4
Key Rule: √a × √b = √(a×b). Example: √4 × √9 = √36 = 2×3 = 6 ✅
Watch out! √(a + b) ≠ √a + √b. √(9+16) ≠ √9 + √16 → √25 ≠ 3+4 → 5 ≠ 7 ❌
Part 4

Real-Life Examples 🇵🇭

1. Tiling a floor. A square floor has area 144 m². Side length = √144 = 12 meters.
2. Storage box. A cubic container has volume 64 dm³. Side = ∛64 = 4 dm.
3. Buying tiles. Square tile area = 0.25 m². Side = √0.25 = 0.5 m or 50 cm.
4. Estimating. A square field is 50 m². Side is between √49 = 7 and √64 = 8 → ~7.07 m.
5. Cubic water tank. Volume = 1000 liters. Side = ∛1000 = 10 dm (since 1 dm³ = 1 L).
Steps: Identify what's being asked → square or cube root? → Is it perfect? Yes = exact value / No = estimate between two perfect roots.
