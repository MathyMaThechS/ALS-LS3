Part 1

📌 What are Like Terms?

Like terms are terms that have the same variable raised to the same power. Only the coefficients can be different!

✅ Like Terms
• 3x and −5x   (same variable x, exponent 1)
• 2y² and 7y²   (same variable y, exponent 2)
• 4 and −8       (constants — no variable)
❌ NOT Like Terms
• 2x and 2y   (different variables)
• 3x and 3x²   (different exponents)
• 4x and 7     (one has variable, one doesn't)

🎯 Identifying Like Terms — 5 Worked Examples

Study each example first. Click Show Solution to see the answer and the reasoning step by step.

Example 1: Are 4x and −7x like terms?
Check the variable part of each term.
Step 1: The first term is 4x. Its variable part is x.
Step 2: The second term is −7x. Its variable part is also x.
Conclusion: YES. Both terms have the same variable and exponent (x¹), so they are like terms.
Example 2: Are 3a² and 3a like terms?
Compare the exponents.
Step 1: In 3a², the exponent of a is 2.
Step 2: In 3a, the exponent of a is 1.
Conclusion: NO. The exponents are different, so these are not like terms.
Example 3: Simplify 5x + 8x.
Combine the like terms.
Step 1: Both terms contain x, so they are like terms.
Step 2: Add the coefficients: 5 + 8 = 13.
Step 3: Keep the variable x.
Answer: 5x + 8x = 13x.
Example 4: Simplify 9y² − 4y².
Subtract the coefficients.
Step 1: Both terms have the same variable part, y².
Step 2: Subtract the coefficients: 9 − 4 = 5.
Step 3: Keep y² unchanged.
Answer: 9y² − 4y² = 5y².
Example 5: Simplify 3x + 4 + 2x − 7.
Group variable terms and constants.
Step 1: Group like terms: (3x + 2x) + (4 − 7).
Step 2: Combine the x-terms: 3x + 2x = 5x.
Step 3: Combine the constants: 4 − 7 = −3.
Answer: 5x − 3. Remember: x-terms and constants are not like terms, so combine them separately.

Part 2

➕ Adding Polynomials

Step 1: Remove parentheses
Step 2: Group like terms together
Step 3: Add the coefficients — keep the variable and exponent the same!

🧩 Worked Examples — 5 Problems

Rule: When adding polynomials, remove the parentheses, group like terms, then add their coefficients. Keep each variable and exponent unchanged.

Example 1: Add (3x + 4) + (2x − 7).
Follow each step.
Step 1 — Remove parentheses: 3x + 4 + 2x − 7.
Step 2 — Group like terms: (3x + 2x) + (4 − 7).
Step 3 — Add coefficients: 3x + 2x = 5x, and 4 − 7 = −3.
Answer: 5x − 3.
Example 2: Add (2x² + 3x − 5) + (4x² − x + 1).
Combine terms with matching powers.
Step 1 — Remove parentheses: 2x² + 3x − 5 + 4x² − x + 1.
Step 2 — Group like terms: (2x² + 4x²) + (3x − x) + (−5 + 1).
Step 3 — Add each group: 6x² + 2x − 4.
Answer: 6x² + 2x − 4.
Example 3: Add (5a + 2b) + (3a − 7b).
Notice that a-terms and b-terms are different groups.
Step 1 — Remove parentheses: 5a + 2b + 3a − 7b.
Step 2 — Group like terms: (5a + 3a) + (2b − 7b).
Step 3 — Add coefficients: 5a + 3a = 8a; 2b − 7b = −5b.
Answer: 8a − 5b.
Example 4: Add (x² − 4x + 6) + (3x² + 2x − 9).
Combine the squared terms, x-terms, and constants separately.
Step 1 — Remove parentheses: x² − 4x + 6 + 3x² + 2x − 9.
Step 2 — Group like terms: (x² + 3x²) + (−4x + 2x) + (6 − 9).
Step 3 — Simplify each group: 4x² − 2x − 3.
Answer: 4x² − 2x − 3.
Example 5: Add (2m² + 3m − 1) + (m² − 5m + 8) + (4m² + m − 2).
There are three polynomials. Group all matching terms.
Step 1 — Remove parentheses: 2m² + 3m − 1 + m² − 5m + 8 + 4m² + m − 2.
Step 2 — Group like terms: (2m² + m² + 4m²) + (3m − 5m + m) + (−1 + 8 − 2).
Step 3 — Add coefficients: 7m² + (−1m) + 5.
Answer: 7m² − m + 5.

Part 3

➖ Subtracting Polynomials

⚠️ MOST IMPORTANT RULE: When subtracting, change the sign of EVERY term in the second polynomial!

Step 1: Distribute the negative sign to ALL terms in the second polynomial
Step 2: Remove parentheses
Step 3: Combine like terms

🧩 Worked Examples — 5 Problems

Rule: Subtracting a polynomial means adding its opposite. Change the sign of every term inside the second set of parentheses, then combine like terms.

Example 1: Subtract (5x + 3) − (2x + 4).
Pay attention to both signs in the second parentheses.
Step 1 — Keep the first polynomial: 5x + 3.
Step 2 — Change every sign in the second polynomial: −(2x + 4) becomes −2x − 4.
Step 3 — Rewrite and group: 5x + 3 − 2x − 4 = (5x − 2x) + (3 − 4).
Step 4 — Simplify: 3x − 1.
Answer: 3x − 1.
Example 2: Subtract (7x² − 4x + 2) − (3x² + 5x − 1).
The negative sign changes +5x to −5x and −1 to +1.
Step 1 — Change every sign in the second polynomial: 3x² + 5x − 1 becomes −3x² − 5x + 1.
Step 2 — Rewrite: 7x² − 4x + 2 − 3x² − 5x + 1.
Step 3 — Group like terms: (7x² − 3x²) + (−4x − 5x) + (2 + 1).
Step 4 — Simplify: 4x² − 9x + 3.
Answer: 4x² − 9x + 3.
Example 3: Subtract (8a + 2b) − (3a − 4b).
Remember that subtracting −4b changes it to +4b.
Step 1 — Change every sign in the second polynomial: 3a − 4b becomes −3a + 4b.
Step 2 — Rewrite: 8a + 2b − 3a + 4b.
Step 3 — Group like terms: (8a − 3a) + (2b + 4b).
Step 4 — Simplify: 5a + 6b.
Answer: 5a + 6b.
Example 4: Subtract (6y² + 2y − 5) − (y² − 3y + 4).
Change the signs of all three terms in the second polynomial.
Step 1 — Change signs: y² − 3y + 4 becomes −y² + 3y − 4.
Step 2 — Rewrite: 6y² + 2y − 5 − y² + 3y − 4.
Step 3 — Group like terms: (6y² − y²) + (2y + 3y) + (−5 − 4).
Step 4 — Simplify: 5y² + 5y − 9.
Answer: 5y² + 5y − 9.
Example 5: Subtract (4m² − 2m + 7) − (6m² + 3m − 5).
The result can have negative coefficients.
Step 1 — Change every sign in the second polynomial: 6m² + 3m − 5 becomes −6m² − 3m + 5.
Step 2 — Rewrite: 4m² − 2m + 7 − 6m² − 3m + 5.
Step 3 — Group like terms: (4m² − 6m²) + (−2m − 3m) + (7 + 5).
Step 4 — Simplify: −2m² − 5m + 12.
Answer: −2m² − 5m + 12.

Review

📚 Lesson Summary

Like Terms
3x + 5x = 8x

Same variables with the same exponents can be combined.

Addition
(2x+3)+(x+4)=3x+7

Remove parentheses and add coefficients of like terms.

Subtraction
(5x+3)−(2x+1)=3x+2

Change the sign of every term in the second polynomial, then combine like terms.

Remember: Never combine unlike terms such as x and x². Combine only terms with matching variable parts.
